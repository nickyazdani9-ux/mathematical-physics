# GTOR 2.0 — Research Audit 03 (5 November 2026)

**Status:** New primary-literature comparisons and independent elementary algebra. Research notes for review only, not GTOR validation.  
**Deduplication:** Audit 01 (3 Nov) already covers phase invariance, normalization, geodesic incompleteness and basic graph-vs-Lorentzian caveats; Audit 02 (4 Nov) already catalogs Hayward, Frolov–Zelnikov, Bonanno, Feng, Liang–Li and Tello-Ortiz. Those studies are not counted below as new discoveries.

## Main delta: horizon topology and what “mass-inflation free” actually means

1. **NEW 3 Nov 2026 PREPRINT:** Chen Lan, Hao Yang, Maxim Khlopov and Maxim Krasnov, *Single-horizon regular black holes and the fate of the inner-horizon instability*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04385 (submitted 3 November 2026; no journal peer review verified). For static spherically symmetric solutions with a regular *point-like* center and an asymptotically static exterior, the sign of the metric function is positive at both ends, so its *sign-changing* zeros occur in even number. By replacing the point-like center with a finite-area core, the authors construct classes with one horizon on a specified patch. They show nonnegative scalar-mode effective potentials for specified families, **not** a generic nonlinear gravitational/semiclassical stability theorem. In particular the global extension of one-sided throats deserves scrutiny.

2. **IMPORTANT PRIOR WORK MISSING IN AUDIT 02:** Raúl Carballo-Rubio, Francesco Di Filippo, Stefano Liberati and Matt Visser, *Mass Inflation without Cauchy Horizons*, **Physical Review Letters 133, 181402**, published **1 Nov 2024**, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.133.181402 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14913. In dynamical spacetime models with slowly evolving *inner trapping horizons*, finite and potentially very large mass amplification can occur **without a global Cauchy horizon**. Therefore the fresh 2026 preprint's *elimination of the standard stationary Cauchy-horizon channel on its single-horizon patch* does **not** imply all dynamical amplification processes vanish. The models/horizon assumptions differ: investigate rather than declare a direct contradiction.

3. **IMPORTANT METHODS CAVEAT TO AUDIT 02:** Francesco Di Filippo, David Kubizňák, Aravindhan Srinivasan, *Mass inflation and thin shells in quasitopological gravity*, **Physical Review D 114, 044061**, published **20 Aug 2026**, https://doi.org/10.1103/ddgs-4ggp. They show standard distribution-theoretic null thin shells are unavailable for the pure-gravity regular solutions they analyze; consequently conventional null-shell mass-inflation derivations do not directly apply. This limits how literally to interpret the April 2026 Frolov–Zelnikov QTG null-shell comparison in Audit 02. **These papers need not refer to identical QTG actions or matter prescriptions.** Compare field equations and distributional junction assumptions before treating them as inconsistent or choosing a result.

## Foundational constraints newly attached to GTOR hypotheses

4. **Relational time (foundational):** Don N. Page and William K. Wootters, *Evolution without evolution: Dynamics described by stationary observables*, **Physical Review D 27, 2885–2892**, published **15 Jun 1983**, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.27.2885. A stationary global quantum state can encode conditional dynamics relative to an internal clock. GTOR's “time from relational flow” needs an operational clock definition, conditional observables and a derivation of effective dynamics; mere ordering of updates is insufficient.

5. **Finite-clock operational ambiguity:** Ladina Hausmann, Alexander Schmidhuber and Esteban Castro-Ruiz, *Measurement events relative to temporal quantum reference frames*, **Quantum 9, 1616**, published **30 Jan 2025**, open access https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2025-01-30-1616 ; https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2025-01-30-1616/. Two ways of defining measurements relative to a clock agree in the ideal-clock limit but can differ for nonideal finite-resource clocks. One formulation admits time-nonlocal or nonunitary effective behavior under assumptions. GTOR must specify which measurement process is meant; conditional correlations alone do not determine a unique time-observable theory.

6. **Causal order vs graph distance:** Sumati Surya, *The causal set approach to quantum gravity*, **Living Reviews in Relativity 22, 5**, published **27 Sep 2019**, open access https://doi.org/10.1007/s41114-019-0023-1. This is a REVIEW of original causal-set results (not claimed as a newly published primary experiment). For suitable distinguishing continuum spacetimes, causal ordering determines conformal Lorentzian geometry, and a volume element fixes the remaining conformal scale. Arbitrary finite graphs with shortest-path metrics do not give a time orientation or Lorentzian light cones. Not every causal set is manifoldlike.

7. **Numerical geometry comparator:** J. Ambjørn, J. Jurkiewicz and R. Loll, *Reconstructing the Universe*, **Physical Review D 72, 064014**, published **22 Sep 2005**, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.72.064014. In causal dynamical triangulations they report simulations exhibiting large-scale four-dimensional features with nontrivial shorter-scale geometry. A conditional model-specific result, not proof GTOR's relational update rule produces spacetime. A related primary analysis is *The spectral dimension of the universe is scale dependent*, **Physical Review Letters 95, 171301** (20 Oct 2005), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.95.171301.

The 2007 Bartlett–Rudolph–Spekkens global/reference-frame review, the Fubini–Study metric and phase-invariance algebra are **already** covered in Audit 02, not repeated as new results. An extra absolute U(1) coordinate alone remains a gauge convention unless GTOR specifies an operationally measurable and consistently composed observable.

## Independent mathematical / numerical checks (toy calculations, NOT physical validation)

**Horizon parity.** In a static spherical Schwarzschild-type chart with metric function f, assume f is continuous, f(0)>0 and f(∞)>0. Each simple (sign-changing) zero flips sign; the number of such crossings is even. Degenerate zeros that do not change sign are a separate case. This is an elementary conditional sign argument; it cannot rule out a one-horizon geometry with a finite-area core or different endpoints.

**Hayward numerical baseline:** f(r) = 1−2Mr²/(r³+2Mℓ²), M=1, ℓ=0.3. Its horizon equation r³−2r²+0.18=0 has two positive numerical roots, r≈0.3281208364 and r≈1.9527982899 (plus one negative algebraic root). This illustrates horizon parity for an explicit regular-point-center metric, not perturbative stability.

**One-sided counterexample to a completeness inference:** Take Schwarzschild restricted to areal radius R=x+a, x≥0, with a=0.5, M=1. Then f(x)=1−2/(x+0.5) has one horizon x=1.5, and the curvature at the finite-radius boundary x=0 is finite (K=48M²/a⁶=3072). But causal geodesics can reach that artificial boundary in finite affine/proper parameter unless an actual extension or physical boundary law is supplied. Thus a one-horizon finite-area patch with bounded scalars **does not, by itself, certify completeness**. This truncated Schwarzschild example is intentionally *not* offered as a nonsingular black-hole solution.

**Bounded coordinate range:** The GTOR draft's r=Lu/(1+u), u>0, covers only 0<r<L. If r is the physical areal radius, this coordinate alone cannot cover any asymptotically flat exterior requiring r→∞. One must add a separate exterior chart and consistent junction/extension or define r differently. As already found in Audit 02, u→∞ also need not be an infinite-affine-distance end.

## Research decision queue (pre-register failure conditions)

1. **Interior topology test:** For any GTOR static spherical ansatz state its areal-radius function Σ(x), lapse f(x), domain, endpoint topology, matter/stress tensor and equations. Count f's sign-changing zeros, compute curvature scalars and frame curvature, and find a maximal causal extension. Failure: unexplained boundary reached in finite affine time, divergence, or inability to match a large-radius asymptotically flat exterior.
2. **Dynamics test:** Identify *global Cauchy* vs *quasi-local inner trapping* horizons separately. Simulate perturbations with a declared matter model, compare shell and smooth-wave computations; in QTG first confirm thin-shell junction rules are mathematically valid. Failure: growth in Misner–Sharp mass or frame curvature beyond preregistered bounds.
3. **Relational-time test:** Construct finite Hilbert spaces for clock C and system S satisfying a constraint (H_C+H_S)|Ψ⟩=0. Compare conditioned probabilities against Schrödinger evolution for ideal clocks; perturb clock resolution and quantify differences between measurement constructions. Failure: clock-dependent predictions without a specified operational protocol or violations of standard benchmark in its known regime.
4. **Emergent-geometry test:** For a specified relational update F, compute order/volume statistics, dimensional estimators and Lorentzian causal reconstruction under coarse graining; compare with causal sets/CDT. Failure: graph's positive distance is passed off as Lorentzian signature without an actual light-cone/clock construction.
